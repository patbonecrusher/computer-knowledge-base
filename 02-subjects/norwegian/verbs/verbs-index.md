# Norwegian Verbs - Complete Reference

Complete conjugation tables for all Norwegian verbs learned so far, including regular verbs, irregular verbs, and modal verbs.

---

## Understanding Verb Types

### Regular Verbs

**Regular verbs** follow predictable conjugation patterns that you can apply to any new regular verb.

**Pattern**:
- Infinitive: **å** + verb stem
- Present: stem + **-er** (or **-r** if stem ends in vowel)
- Past: stem + **-et/-te/-dde** (depends on stem ending)
- Past Participle: same as past tense

**Example**: å snakke (to speak)
- Infinitive: å **snakke**
- Present: **snakker** (snakk + er)
- Past: **snakket** (snakk + et)
- Past Participle: **snakket**

Once you know the pattern, you can conjugate any regular verb!

### Irregular Verbs

**Irregular verbs** have unique conjugations that don't follow the standard patterns. Each irregular verb must be **memorized individually**.

**Example**: å være (to be)
- Infinitive: å **være**
- Present: **er** ❌ (NOT "værer")
- Past: **var** ❌ (NOT "været")
- Past Participle: **vært** ❌ (NOT "varet")

There's no pattern to predict these forms - you must memorize them!

### Modal Verbs

**Modal verbs** are special helping verbs with unique characteristics:
- Same form for **all persons** (jeg kan, du kan, vi kan)
- Followed by infinitive **without "å"**
- Rarely use infinitive form themselves
- No imperative form

**Example**: kan (can)
- Present: **kan** (same for all persons)
- Past: **kunne**
- Usage: Jeg **kan snakke** norsk. (NOT "kan å snakke")

### Key Difference Summary

| Type | Pattern | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Regular** | Follows predictable rules | å snakke → snakker → snakket |
| **Irregular** | Must be memorized | å være → er → var → vært |
| **Modal** | Same for all persons + bare infinitive | kan → kunne (usage: kan snakke) |

## Verb Patterns

### Regular Verb Pattern (-er verbs)

Most Norwegian verbs follow this pattern:

**Infinitive**: å + verb stem
**Present**: verb stem + -er (or -r if stem ends in vowel)
**Past**: verb stem + -et/-te/-dde
**Past Participle**: same as past tense

**Examples**:
- å snakke → snakker → snakket → snakket
- å like → liker → likte → likt
- å bo → bor → bodde → bodd

### Modal Verb Pattern

Modal verbs have a unique pattern:

**Infinitive**: å + modal (rarely used)
**Present**: single form for all persons (kan, må, skal, vil, bør)
**Past**: kunne, måtte, skulle, ville, burde
**No Past Participle**: modal verbs don't have past participles

**Usage**: Modal verb + infinitive **without "å"**

**Examples**:
- Jeg **kan snakke** norsk. (I can speak Norwegian.)
- Du **må gå** nå. (You must go now.)
- Vi **skal bo** i Oslo. (We're going to live in Oslo.)

**Important characteristics**:
- Same form for all persons (jeg kan, du kan, vi kan)
- Followed by bare infinitive (no "å")
- No imperative form
- See [[../modal-verbs|Modal Verbs Guide]] for complete explanation

## Tense Overview

Each verb page includes complete conjugation tables for:

### 1. Presens (Present Tense)
Current actions, states, and habitual actions

### 2. Preteritum (Past Tense)
Completed actions in the past

### 3. Presens Perfektum (Present Perfect)
Actions that started in the past and continue or have relevance to the present
- Pattern: **har** + past participle

### 4. Framtid (Future)
Future actions and intentions
- Pattern: **skal/vil** + infinitive

### 5. Imperativ (Imperative)
Commands and instructions

## Quick Reference: Most Common Verbs

### Top 5 Essential Verbs

1. **[[å-være|å være]]** (to be) - Most important verb in Norwegian
   - Jeg **er** student.

2. **[[å-ha|å ha]]** (to have) - Second most important
   - Jeg **har** en bil.

3. **[[å-gjøre|å gjøre]]** (to do/make) - Very versatile
   - Hva **gjør** du?

4. **[[å-gå|å gå]]** (to go) - Common for movement
   - Jeg **går** til skolen.

5. **[[å-komme|å komme]]** (to come) - Essential for communication
   - Jeg **kommer** fra Canada.

### Essential Modal Verbs

1. **kan** (can) - Ability, permission
   - Jeg **kan snakke** norsk.

2. **må** (must) - Obligation, necessity
   - Du **må gå** nå.

3. **skal** (shall/going to) - Plans, future
   - Vi **skal bo** i Oslo.

4. **vil** (will/want to) - Desire, predictions
   - Jeg **vil lære** norsk.

5. **bør** (should) - Advice, recommendations
   - Du **bør snakke** norsk.

See [[../modal-verbs|Modal Verbs Guide]] for complete explanations.

## Negation

To make any verb negative, add **ikke** after the conjugated verb:

- Jeg **er ikke** norsk. (I am not Norwegian.)
- Vi **har ikke** bil. (We don't have a car.)
- Hun **snakker ikke** engelsk. (She doesn't speak English.)
- De **går ikke** til skolen. (They don't go to school.)

---

## Future Tense with Modal Verbs

Norwegian uses modal verbs **skal** and **vil** for future tense. See [[../modal-verbs|Modal Verbs Guide]] for details.

### skal vs vil

**skal** - will (planning, obligation, scheduled future)
- Jeg **skal** gå til skolen. (I'm going to go to school.)
- Vi **skal** bo i Norge. (We're going to live in Norway.)

**vil** - will (wanting, intention, predictions)
- Jeg **vil** snakke norsk. (I want to speak Norwegian.)
- Det **vil** være fint. (It will be nice.)
