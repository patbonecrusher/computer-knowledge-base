# å koste (to cost)

**Type**: Regular verb
**Infinitive**: å koste

**Meaning**: To cost

**Important Note**: This verb is usually used in 3rd person (things cost, not people)

---

## Presens (Present Tense)

| Person | Norsk | English |
|--------|-------|---------|
| jeg | koster | I cost |
| du | koster | you cost |
| han/hun/det | koster | he/she/it costs |
| vi | koster | we cost |
| dere | koster | you cost (plural) |
| de | koster | they cost |

### Eksempler (Examples):
- Boken **koster** hundre kroner. (The book costs 100 crowns.)
- Hvor mye **koster** det? (How much does it cost?)
- Billetten **koster** femti kroner. (The ticket costs 50 crowns.)
- Eplene **koster** ti kroner per kilo. (The apples cost 10 crowns per kilo.)
- Dette **koster** for mye. (This costs too much.)

## Preteritum (Past Tense)

| Person | Norsk | English |
|--------|-------|---------|
| jeg | kostet | I cost |
| du | kostet | you cost |
| han/hun/det | kostet | he/she/it cost |
| vi | kostet | we cost |
| dere | kostet | you cost (plural) |
| de | kostet | they cost |

### Eksempler:
- Billetten **kostet** hundre kroner. (The ticket cost 100 crowns.)
- Hvor mye **kostet** det? (How much did it cost?)
- Middagen **kostet** to hundre kroner. (The dinner cost 200 crowns.)
- Det **kostet** mer enn jeg trodde. (It cost more than I thought.)

## Presens perfektum (Present Perfect)

**Pattern**: har + kostet

| Person | Norsk | English |
|--------|-------|---------|
| jeg | har kostet | I have cost |
| du | har kostet | you have cost |
| han/hun/det | har kostet | he/she/it has cost |
| vi | har kostet | we have cost |
| dere | har kostet | you have cost (plural) |
| de | har kostet | they have cost |

### Eksempler:
- Det **har kostet** meg mye penger. (It has cost me a lot of money.)
- Hvor mye **har det kostet**? (How much has it cost?)
- Reisen **har kostet** tusen kroner. (The trip has cost 1000 crowns.)

| Person | Norsk | English |
|--------|-------|---------|

| du | skal koste / vil koste | you will cost |
| han/hun/det | skal koste / vil koste | he/she/it will cost |

### Eksempler:
- Det **vil koste** mye. (It will cost a lot.)
- Hvor mye **skal det koste**? (How much will it cost?)
- Billetten **kommer til å koste** hundre kroner. (The ticket is going to cost 100 crowns.)

---

## Imperativ (Imperative)

**kost** - cost! (rarely used in imperative)

---

## Nektelse (Negation)

Add **ikke** after the verb:

- Det **koster ikke** mye. (It doesn't cost much.)
- Billetten **kostet ikke** så mye. (The ticket didn't cost that much.)
- Det **har ikke kostet** meg noe. (It hasn't cost me anything.)

---

## Nyttige Uttrykk (Useful Expressions)

- **Hva koster det?** - What does it cost? / How much is it?
  - **Hva koster** kaffen? (How much does the coffee cost?)
- **Det koster...** - It costs...
  - **Det koster** ti kroner. (It costs 10 crowns.)
- **koste mye** - to cost a lot
  - Det **koster mye**. (It costs a lot.)
- **koste lite** - to cost little
  - Det **koster lite**. (It costs little.)
- **koste ingenting** - to cost nothing
  - Det **koster ingenting**. (It costs nothing.)
- **koste for mye** - to cost too much
  - Det **koster for mye**. (It costs too much.)

---

## Common Uses

**Asking prices:**
- **Hva koster** det? (What does it cost? / How much is it?)
- **Hvor mye koster** billetten? (How much does the ticket cost?)
- **Hva koster** kaffen her? (How much does coffee cost here?)

**Stating prices:**
- Det **koster** hundre kroner. (It costs 100 crowns.)
- Boken **koster** to hundre kroner. (The book costs 200 crowns.)
- Lunsj **koster** hundre og femti kroner. (Lunch costs 150 crowns.)

**Figurative meaning (to cost effort/sacrifice):**
- Det **kostet** meg mye energi. (It cost me a lot of energy.)
- Det **koster** å være berømt. (It costs to be famous / There's a price to fame.)

---

## Prepositions with "koste"

**Generally, "koste" doesn't require prepositions when stating the price:**
- Det **koster** hundre kroner. (It costs 100 crowns.)

**But can be used with prepositions for other meanings:**
- **koste på seg** - to treat oneself to
  - Jeg **koster på meg** en kaffe. (I treat myself to a coffee.)

---

## Common Questions and Answers

**Question:**
- **Hva koster det?** (What does it cost?)
- **Hvor mye koster det?** (How much does it cost?)
- **Hva koster kaffen?** (How much does coffee cost?)

**Answer:**
- Det **koster** ti kroner. (It costs 10 crowns.)
- Den **koster** hundre kroner. (It costs 100 crowns.)
- Kaffen **koster** førti kroner. (Coffee costs 40 crowns.)

---

## Price Ranges

**Cheap:**
- Det **koster** ikke mye. (It doesn't cost much.)
- Det er billig, det **koster** bare ti kroner. (It's cheap, it only costs 10 crowns.)

**Expensive:**
- Det **koster** veldig mye! (It costs very much!)
- Det **koster** fem hundre kroner! (It costs 500 crowns!)

**Free:**
- Det **koster** ingenting, det er gratis. (It costs nothing, it's free.)

---

## Cultural Context: Typical Norwegian Prices

- En kaffe **koster** 30-50 kr (A coffee costs 30-50 crowns)
- En lunsj **koster** 100-150 kr (A lunch costs 100-150 crowns)
- En middag **koster** 200-400 kr (A dinner costs 200-400 crowns)
- En bussbillett **koster** 40-60 kr (A bus ticket costs 40-60 crowns)
- En kinobillett **koster** 120-150 kr (A cinema ticket costs 120-150 crowns)

**Important phrase:**
- Norge **er dyrt**! (Norway is expensive!)

## Figurative Uses

**"å koste" can also mean to require effort or sacrifice:**

- Det **koster** å være leder. (It costs to be a leader / Being a leader comes at a price.)
- Suksess **koster** hardt arbeid. (Success costs hard work.)
- Det **kostet** ham helsen. (It cost him his health.)
- Det **koster** meg mye energi. (It costs me a lot of energy.)
