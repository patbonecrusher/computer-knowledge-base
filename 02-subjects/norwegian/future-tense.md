# Future Tense in Norwegian

Complete guide to expressing future actions in Norwegian.

---

## Present Continuous vs Present vs Future

Before diving into future tense, it's important to understand how Norwegian differs from English in expressing ongoing actions.

### "I am doing something" (Present Continuous)

**In English**: "I **am doing** homework" (present continuous - happening right now)
**In Norwegian**: "Jeg **gjør** lekser" (simple present - same form!)

**Key difference**: Norwegian doesn't have a separate present continuous tense. The **simple present tense** covers both:
- Habitual actions: "I do homework" → Jeg **gjør** lekser
- Ongoing actions: "I am doing homework" → Jeg **gjør** lekser (same!)

### How to emphasize "right now" in Norwegian:

If you want to stress that something is happening **right now**, add time words:

- Jeg **gjør** lekser **nå**. (I'm doing homework now.)
- Jeg **snakker** norsk **akkurat nå**. (I'm speaking Norwegian right now.)
- Hun **går** til skolen **nå**. (She's going to school now.)

### Alternative: "holder på med" (emphasis on ongoing)

For stronger emphasis that something is ongoing:

**Pattern**: holder på med å + infinitive OR holder på å + infinitive

- Jeg **holder på med** leksene. (I'm busy with the homework / I'm doing the homework.)
- Jeg **holder på å gjøre** lekser. (I'm in the process of doing homework.)
- Vi **holder på å snakke** norsk. (We're speaking Norwegian / We're in the process of speaking Norwegian.)

**Note**: This is more emphatic and less common in everyday speech. Usually simple present is enough!

### Summary:

| English | Norwegian | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| I do homework | Jeg gjør lekser | Habitual |
| I am doing homework | Jeg gjør lekser | Same form! |
| I am doing homework (now) | Jeg gjør lekser nå | Add "nå" for emphasis |
| I am doing homework (ongoing) | Jeg holder på å gjøre lekser | Very emphatic |

## Overview

Norwegian doesn't have a single "future tense" like English. Instead, there are **four main ways** to express future:

1. **skal** + infinitive (planned/scheduled)
2. **vil** + infinitive (want to/predictions)
3. **kommer til å** + infinitive (going to)
4. **Present tense** + time expression (implied future)

---

## 1. skal + infinitive

**Pattern**: skal + verb infinitive

**Meaning**: going to, will (planned or scheduled)

### When to use:
- ✅ Plans and intentions
- ✅ Scheduled events
- ✅ Obligations
- ✅ Definite arrangements
- ✅ What you're "going to" do

### Conjugation:

| Person  | Norsk | Example                       |
| ------- | ----- | ----------------------------- |
| jeg     | skal  | Jeg **skal gå** til skolen.   |
| du      | skal  | Du **skal snakke** norsk.     |
| han/hun | skal  | Hun **skal bo** i Oslo.       |
| vi      | skal  | Vi **skal gjøre** lekser.     |
| dere    | skal  | Dere **skal komme** i morgen. |
| de      | skal  | De **skal ha** møte.          |

### Examples:

**Plans:**
- Jeg **skal gå** til skolen i morgen. (I'm going to go to school tomorrow.)
- Vi **skal bo** i Norge. (We're going to live in Norway.)

**Scheduled events:**
- Møtet **skal være** klokken tre. (The meeting will be at 3 o'clock.)
- Bussen **skal komme** snart. (The bus is going to come soon.)

**Obligations:**
- Jeg **skal gjøre** lekser. (I have to do homework / I'm going to do homework.)
- Du **skal være** her klokken åtte. (You need to be here at 8 o'clock.)

**Questions:**
- Hva **skal du gjøre** i kveld? (What are you going to do tonight?)
- Når **skal vi gå**? (When are we going to leave?)
- **Skal vi gå** til Oslo? (Shall we go to Oslo?)

---

## 2. vil + infinitive

**Pattern**: vil + verb infinitive

**Meaning**: want to, will (desire or prediction)

### When to use:
- ✅ Wanting/desiring to do something
- ✅ Willingness
- ✅ Predictions
- ✅ Spontaneous decisions
- ✅ Offers

### Conjugation:

| Person | Norsk | Example |
|--------|-------|---------|
| jeg | vil | Jeg **vil snakke** norsk. |
| du | vil | Du **vil like** det. |
| han/hun | vil | Hun **vil komme**. |
| vi | vil | Vi **vil gå** nå. |
| dere | vil | Dere **vil ha** det gøy. |
| de | vil | De **vil gjøre** det. |

### Examples:

**Wanting/Desire:**
- Jeg **vil snakke** norsk. (I want to speak Norwegian.)
- **Vil du ha** kaffe? (Do you want coffee?)
- Vi **vil bo** i Oslo. (We want to live in Oslo.)

**Predictions:**
- Jeg tror det **vil gå** bra. (I think it will go well.)
- Det **vil være** fint i morgen. (It will be nice tomorrow.)
- Hun **vil like** Norge. (She will like Norway.)

**Offers:**
- Jeg **vil gjøre** det for deg. (I'll do it for you.)
- **Vil du hjelpe** meg? (Will you help me?)

**Spontaneous decisions:**
- Ok, jeg **vil gjøre** det. (Ok, I'll do it.)
- Vi **vil komme**! (We'll come!)

---

## 3. kommer til å + infinitive

**Pattern**: kommer til å + verb infinitive

**Meaning**: going to, will (prediction/certainty)

### When to use:
- ✅ Predictions based on evidence
- ✅ Things that will definitely happen
- ✅ More certain future events
- ✅ Conclusions about the future

### Conjugation:

| Person | Norsk | Example |
|--------|-------|---------|
| jeg | kommer til å | Jeg **kommer til å snakke** norsk. |
| du | kommer til å | Du **kommer til å like** det. |
| han/hun | kommer til å | Hun **kommer til å bo** i Oslo. |
| vi | kommer til å | Vi **kommer til å gjøre** det. |
| dere | kommer til å | Dere **kommer til å ha** det gøy. |
| de | kommer til å | De **kommer til å komme**. |

### Examples:

**Predictions with evidence:**
- Se på skyene! Det **kommer til å** regne. (Look at the clouds! It's going to rain.)
- Jeg **kommer til å** like Norge. (I'm going to like Norway.)

**Certainty about future:**
- Det **kommer til å være** fint. (It's going to be nice.)
- Vi **kommer til å snakke** norsk. (We're going to speak Norwegian.)

**Conclusions:**
- Du **kommer til å være** sliten. (You're going to be tired.)
- Dette **kommer til å ta** tid. (This is going to take time.)

---

## 4. Present Tense + Time Expression

**Pattern**: present tense verb + time word

**Meaning**: Implied future through context

### When to use:
- ✅ Very common in casual Norwegian!
- ✅ When time expression makes future clear
- ✅ Near future events
- ✅ Scheduled/planned activities

### Time expressions that signal future:
- **i morgen** (tomorrow)
- **i kveld** (tonight)
- **senere** (later)
- **på mandag** (on Monday)
- **neste uke** (next week)
- **snart** (soon)

### Examples:

- Jeg **går** til skolen **i morgen**. (I'm going to school tomorrow.)
- Hva **gjør du** **i kveld**? (What are you doing tonight?)
- Vi **snakker** **senere**. (We'll talk later.)
- Han **kommer** **på mandag**. (He's coming on Monday.)
- De **har** møte **neste uke**. (They have a meeting next week.)

---

## Comparison: skal vs vil vs kommer til å

| Expression | Best for | Example |
|------------|----------|---------|
| **skal** | Plans, schedules, obligations | Jeg **skal gå** til skolen. (I'm going to go to school.) |
| **vil** | Wants, predictions, offers | Jeg **vil snakke** norsk. (I want to speak Norwegian.) |
| **kommer til å** | Certain predictions | Det **kommer til å** regne. (It's going to rain.) |
| **Present + time** | Clear context | Jeg **går** til skolen **i morgen**. (I'm going to school tomorrow.) |

## Side-by-Side Examples

### Same sentence, different helpers:

**Jeg skal snakke norsk.**
- I'm going to speak Norwegian. (It's my plan)

**Jeg vil snakke norsk.**
- I want to speak Norwegian. (It's my desire)

**Jeg kommer til å snakke norsk.**
- I'm going to speak Norwegian. (I'm certain/it will happen)

**Jeg snakker norsk i morgen.**
- I'm speaking Norwegian tomorrow. (Scheduled/planned)

## Common Questions and How to Ask Them

### What are you going to do?

**Hva skal du gjøre?** (most common - asking about plans)
- Hva **skal du gjøre** i kveld?
- Hva **skal du gjøre** i morgen?
- Hva **skal du gjøre** på mandag?

**Hva vil du gjøre?** (asking what they want to do)
- Hva **vil du gjøre**?

**Hva gjør du i kveld?** (present tense + time = future)
- Hva **gjør du** i kveld?
- Hva **gjør du** i morgen?

### When are you coming?

**Når skal du komme?** (asking about plans)
- Når **skal du komme**?

**Når kommer du?** (present tense, context clear)
- Når **kommer du**?

### Will you help me?

**Vil du hjelpe meg?** (asking willingness)
- **Vil du hjelpe** meg?

**Skal du hjelpe meg?** (asking if it's planned)
- **Skal du hjelpe** meg?

## Negation with Future

Add **ikke** after the helper verb:

- Jeg **skal ikke** gå. (I'm not going to go.)
- Vi **vil ikke** gjøre det. (We won't do it / We don't want to do it.)
- Hun **kommer ikke til å** like det. (She's not going to like it.)
- Jeg **går ikke** til skolen i morgen. (I'm not going to school tomorrow.)

---

## Special Cases

### "Shall we...?" = Skal vi...?

**Skal vi** is commonly used for suggestions:
- **Skal vi gå**? (Shall we go?)
- **Skal vi spise**? (Shall we eat?)
- **Skal vi snakke** norsk? (Shall we speak Norwegian?)

### Modal verbs + infinitive (also express future)

**må** (must, have to) + infinitive:
- Jeg **må gå** nå. (I have to go now.)

**kan** (can, may) + infinitive:
- Jeg **kan komme** i morgen. (I can come tomorrow.)

**bør** (should, ought to) + infinitive:
- Du **bør snakke** norsk. (You should speak Norwegian.)

## Practice Sentences

### Translate to Norwegian:

1. I'm going to speak Norwegian tomorrow. (plan)
2. I want to live in Norway. (desire)
3. It's going to rain. (prediction with evidence)
4. What are you doing tonight? (present + time)
5. We will go to Oslo on Monday. (plan)

### Answers:

1. Jeg **skal snakke** norsk i morgen.
2. Jeg **vil bo** i Norge.
3. Det **kommer til å** regne.
4. Hva **gjør du** i kveld? (or: Hva **skal du gjøre** i kveld?)
5. Vi **skal gå** til Oslo på mandag. (or: Vi **går** til Oslo på mandag.)

## Quick Decision Guide

**Use skal when:**
- You've made plans
- It's scheduled
- You're talking about what's going to happen

**Use vil when:**
- You want to do something
- You're making an offer
- You're predicting based on opinion

**Use kommer til å when:**
- You see evidence something will happen
- You're very certain about the future
- You're making a conclusion

**Use present tense when:**
- Time expression makes it clear
- It's casual conversation
- Near future
