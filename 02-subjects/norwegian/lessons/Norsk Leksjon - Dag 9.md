## Del 2: Grammatikk (7 minutter)

### Telling fra 21-99

Numbers from 21-99 follow a pattern: [tens] + [ones]

- **tjueen** (21) - twenty-one
- **tjueto** (22) - twenty-two
- **trettitre** (33) - thirty-three
- **førtifire** (44) - forty-four
- **femtifem** (55) - fifty-five
- **nittini** (99) - ninety-nine

**Note**: The numbers are written as one word!

### Telling over 100

- **hundre** - one hundred
- **hundre og én** - 101
- **to hundre** - 200
- **tre hundre og førti** - 340
- **tusen** - 1000
- **to tusen** - 2000
- **én million** - 1,000,000

### Én vs Ett (One)

**én** - used with masculine/feminine nouns
- **én mann** (one man)
- **én bok** (one book)

**ett** - used with neuter nouns
- **ett hus** (one house)
- **ett eple** (one apple)

### Verbet "å telle" (to count) - Regular

| Person | Presens | Preteritum |
|--------|---------|------------|
| jeg | teller | talte |
| du | teller | talte |
| han/hun/det | teller | talte |
| vi | teller | talte |
| dere | teller | talte |
| de | teller | talte |

**Eksempler:**
- Jeg **teller** til ti. (I count to ten.)
- Hun **talte** pengene. (She counted the money.)

### Verbet "å koste" (to cost) - Regular

| Person | Presens | Preteritum |
|--------|---------|------------|
| jeg | koster | kostet |
| du | koster | kostet |
| han/hun/det | koster | kostet |
| vi | koster | kostet |
| dere | koster | kostet |
| de | koster | kostet |

**Eksempler:**
- Boken **koster** hundre kroner. (The book costs 100 crowns.)
- Billetten **kostet** femti kroner. (The ticket cost 50 crowns.)

**Note**: "å koste" is usually used in 3rd person (things cost, not people)

### Verbet "å betale" (to pay) - Regular

| Person | Presens | Preteritum |
|--------|---------|------------|
| jeg | betaler | betalte |
| du | betaler | betalte |
| han/hun/det | betaler | betalte |
| vi | betaler | betalte |
| dere | betaler | betalte |
| de | betaler | betalte |

**Eksempler:**
- Jeg **betaler** med kort. (I pay with card.)
- Vi **betalte** tusen kroner. (We paid 1000 crowns.)

### Pris uttrykk (Price expressions)

**Hva koster det?** - What does it cost? / How much is it?

**Det koster...** - It costs...
- Det koster **ti kroner**. (It costs 10 crowns.)
- Det koster **hundre og femti kroner**. (It costs 150 crowns.)

**Kroner og øre** (Crowns and øre):
- **25 kroner** (25 crowns)
- **25,50 kroner** or **tjuefem kroner og femti øre** (25 crowns and 50 øre)

### Alder (Age)

**Hvor gammel er du?** - How old are you?

**Jeg er... år gammel** - I am... years old
- Jeg er **tjue år gammel**. (I am 20 years old.)
- Hun er **trettifem år**. (She is 35 years.) - "gammel" often omitted

**Eksempler:**
- Barnet er **fem år** gammelt. (The child is 5 years old.)
- Min far er **seksti år**. (My father is 60 years.)

### Telefonnummer (Phone numbers)

Phone numbers are usually said in pairs:
- **98 76 54 32** = nittåtte syttiseks femtifire trettito

### Datoer (Dates)

Use ordinal numbers for dates:
- **den første januar** - January 1st
- **den andre mai** - May 2nd
- **den syttende mai** - May 17th (Norwegian Constitution Day!)

## Kulturnotat (Cultural note)

### Norwegian Currency and Prices

**Kroner and Øre:**
- Norwegian currency is the **krone** (crown), plural **kroner**
- 1 krone = 100 øre (though øre coins are rarely used anymore)
- Symbol: kr or NOK (Norwegian Krone)

**Typical Prices in Norway (2024):**
- Coffee: 30-50 kr
- Lunch: 100-150 kr
- Dinner at restaurant: 200-400 kr
- Bus ticket: 40-60 kr
- Cinema ticket: 120-150 kr

**Norway is expensive!** Compared to many countries, prices in Norway are high because of high wages and standard of living.

**Tipping:**
- Not as common as in USA
- Service is usually included
- Rounding up or 5-10% for good service is appreciated

**Payment:**
- Norwegians use cards for almost everything
- Even small purchases (a coffee) are usually paid by card
- Cash (**kontant**) is becoming rare
- Mobile payment apps (Vipps) are very popular

**Important phrase:**
- **Kan jeg betale med kort?** (Can I pay with card?) - Almost always "yes"!

### Important Dates with Ordinal Numbers

**17. mai** (den syttende mai) - Norway's Constitution Day (National Day)
- The most important national holiday
- Everyone wears traditional costumes (bunad)
- Parades, flags, ice cream, hot dogs!
- Celebrates Norwegian independence (1814)

**1. januar** (den første januar) - New Year's Day
**1. mai** (den første mai) - Labour Day
**25. desember** (den tjuefemte desember) - Christmas Day

## Grammatikknotat (Grammar note)

### Pattern for Numbers 21-99

Norwegian combines tens and ones into **one word**:

**tjue** (20) + **én** (1) = **tjueen** (21)
**tretti** (30) + **to** (2) = **trettito** (32)
**førti** (40) + **tre** (3) = **førtitre** (43)

This is different from English where we say "twenty-one" (two words).

### Using "og" (and) in larger numbers

For numbers over 100, use **og** (and) between hundreds and the rest:

- **101** = hundre **og** én
- **250** = to hundre **og** femti
- **1001** = tusen **og** én

But NOT within the tens/ones:
- ~~tjue og én~~ ❌
- **tjueen** ✓

### Ordinal Numbers Pattern

Most ordinal numbers follow a pattern:

**Cardinal** → **Ordinal**
- fire (4) → fjerde (4th)
- fem (5) → femte (5th)
- seks (6) → sjette (6th)
- syv (7) → syvende (7th)
- åtte (8) → åttende (8th)

**Irregular ones to memorize:**
- én → **første** (1st)
- to → **andre** (2nd)
- tre → **tredje** (3rd)

### Gender Agreement with "én/ett"

Remember that "one" changes based on noun gender:

**Masculine/Feminine (én):**
- én krone
- én dag
- én bok

**Neuter (ett):**
- ett år
- ett eple
- ett hus

For all other numbers (2-∞), no gender agreement needed!

## Lekse til i morgen:

1. Memorize numbers 0-20 (say them out loud!)
2. Practice counting by tens: 10, 20, 30... 100
3. Learn to ask and answer "Hvor gammel er du?" (How old are you?)
4. Practice saying prices: "Det koster ___ kroner"
5. Learn the conjugations of å telle, å koste, å betale
6. Practice phone numbers by saying them in pairs

**Eksempel øvelse:**

Practice saying your age, family members' ages:
- Jeg er **trettitre år**.
- Min mor er **seksti år**.
- Min bror er **tjueåtte år**.

Practice asking prices:
- **Hva koster** kaffen? - Den koster **førti kroner**.
- **Hva koster** billetten? - Den koster **hundre kroner**.

**Lykke til!** (Good luck!)
