# Modal Verbs in Norwegian

Complete guide to Norwegian modal verbs (helping verbs).

---

## What are Modal Verbs?

Modal verbs are helper verbs that express:
- Ability (can)
- Permission (may)
- Obligation (must)
- Necessity (have to)
- Willingness (will)
- Advice (should)
- Future (going to)

**Pattern**: Modal verb + infinitive (without "å")

**Example**: Jeg **kan snakke** norsk. (I **can speak** Norwegian.)

---

## The Main Modal Verbs

| Infinitive | Present | Past | English |
|------------|---------|------|---------|
| å kunne | kan | kunne | can / to be able to |
| å måtte | må | måtte | must / have to |
| å skulle | skal | skulle | shall / will / going to |
| å ville | vil | ville | will / want to |
| å burde | bør | burde | should / ought to |

**Important**: Modal verbs have the **same form for all persons** in present tense!

---

## 1. kan (can / to be able to)

**Infinitive**: å kunne
**Present**: kan
**Past**: kunne

### Present Tense Conjugation:

| Person | Norsk | English |
|--------|-------|---------|
| jeg | kan | I can |
| du | kan | you can |
| han/hun | kan | he/she can |
| vi | kan | we can |
| dere | kan | you can (plural) |
| de | kan | they can |

### When to use:
- ✅ Ability (physical/mental capability)
- ✅ Permission (asking/giving)
- ✅ Possibility
- ✅ Knowing how to do something

### Examples:

**Ability:**
- Jeg **kan snakke** norsk. (I can speak Norwegian.)
- Hun **kan gå** langt. (She can walk far.)

**Permission:**
- **Kan jeg gå** hjem? (Can I go home?)
- Du **kan komme** i morgen. (You can come tomorrow.)

**Possibility:**
- Det **kan være** sant. (It can be true / It might be true.)
- Vi **kan gjøre** det senere. (We can do it later.)

**Knowing how:**
- Jeg **kan snakke** tre språk. (I can speak three languages.)
- **Kan du snakke** engelsk? (Can you speak English?)

### Past tense:
- Jeg **kunne snakke** norsk. (I could speak Norwegian.)
- Vi **kunne gå**. (We could go.)

### Negation:
- Jeg **kan ikke snakke** norsk. (I can't speak Norwegian.)
- Hun **kunne ikke komme**. (She couldn't come.)

---

## 2. må (must / have to)

**Infinitive**: å måtte
**Present**: må
**Past**: måtte

### Present Tense Conjugation:

| Person | Norsk | English |
|--------|-------|---------|
| jeg | må | I must / I have to |
| du | må | you must / you have to |
| han/hun | må | he/she must / has to |
| vi | må | we must / we have to |
| dere | må | you must / you have to (plural) |
| de | må | they must / they have to |

### When to use:
- ✅ Obligation (external or internal)
- ✅ Necessity
- ✅ Requirement
- ✅ Strong advice

### Examples:

**Obligation/Necessity:**
- Jeg **må gå** nå. (I must go now / I have to go now.)
- Vi **må snakke** norsk. (We have to speak Norwegian.)

**Requirement:**
- Du **må ha** pass. (You must have a passport.)
- Man **må være** over 18. (One must be over 18.)

**Strong conclusion:**
- Det **må være** sant. (It must be true.)
- Hun **må bo** i Oslo. (She must live in Oslo.)

### Past tense:
- Jeg **måtte gå**. (I had to go.)
- Vi **måtte snakke** norsk. (We had to speak Norwegian.)

### Negation:
- Jeg **må ikke gå**. (I must not go / I'm not allowed to go.)
  - **Warning**: "må ikke" = must not (prohibition), not "don't have to"!

**For "don't have to"** use: **trenger ikke å** or **behøver ikke å**
- Jeg **trenger ikke å gå**. (I don't have to go / I don't need to go.)

---

## 3. skal (shall / will / going to)

**Infinitive**: å skulle
**Present**: skal
**Past**: skulle

### Present Tense Conjugation:

| Person | Norsk | English |
|--------|-------|---------|
| jeg | skal | I shall / I'm going to |
| du | skal | you shall / you're going to |
| han/hun | skal | he/she shall / is going to |
| vi | skal | we shall / we're going to |
| dere | skal | you shall / you're going to (plural) |
| de | skal | they shall / they're going to |

### When to use:
- ✅ Future plans
- ✅ Intentions
- ✅ Scheduled events
- ✅ Promises
- ✅ Suggestions (Skal vi...?)

### Examples:

**Future plans:**
- Jeg **skal gå** til skolen i morgen. (I'm going to go to school tomorrow.)
- Vi **skal bo** i Norge. (We're going to live in Norway.)

**Intentions:**
- Jeg **skal snakke** norsk. (I'm going to speak Norwegian.)
- Hun **skal gjøre** lekser. (She's going to do homework.)

**Suggestions:**
- **Skal vi gå**? (Shall we go?)
- **Skal vi snakke** norsk? (Shall we speak Norwegian?)

**Questions about plans:**
- Hva **skal du gjøre**? (What are you going to do?)
- Når **skal du komme**? (When are you going to come?)

### Past tense (skulle):

**Past plans:**
- Jeg **skulle gå**. (I was going to go.)
- Vi **skulle komme**. (We were going to come.)

**Conditional (would):**
- Jeg **skulle like** å bo i Oslo. (I would like to live in Oslo.)
- Hva **skulle du gjøre**? (What would you do?)

### Negation:
- Jeg **skal ikke gå**. (I'm not going to go.)
- Vi **skulle ikke komme**. (We weren't going to come.)

---

## 4. vil (will / want to)

**Infinitive**: å ville
**Present**: vil
**Past**: ville

### Present Tense Conjugation:

| Person | Norsk | English |
|--------|-------|---------|
| jeg | vil | I will / I want to |
| du | vil | you will / you want to |
| han/hun | vil | he/she will / wants to |
| vi | vil | we will / we want to |
| dere | vil | you will / you want to (plural) |
| de | vil | they will / they want to |

### When to use:
- ✅ Wanting/desiring
- ✅ Willingness
- ✅ Predictions
- ✅ Offers
- ✅ Spontaneous decisions

### Examples:

**Wanting:**
- Jeg **vil snakke** norsk. (I want to speak Norwegian.)
- **Vil du ha** kaffe? (Do you want coffee?)
- Vi **vil bo** i Norge. (We want to live in Norway.)

**Willingness/Offers:**
- Jeg **vil gjøre** det for deg. (I'll do it for you.)
- **Vil du hjelpe** meg? (Will you help me?)

**Predictions:**
- Jeg tror det **vil gå** bra. (I think it will go well.)
- Det **vil være** fint. (It will be nice.)

**Questions:**
- Hva **vil du gjøre**? (What do you want to do?)
- **Vil du komme**? (Do you want to come? / Will you come?)

### Past tense (ville):

**Wanted to:**
- Jeg **ville gå**. (I wanted to go.)
- Hun **ville snakke** norsk. (She wanted to speak Norwegian.)

**Conditional (would):**
- Jeg **ville gjøre** det. (I would do it.)
- Hva **ville du gjøre**? (What would you do?)

### Negation:
- Jeg **vil ikke gå**. (I don't want to go / I won't go.)
- Hun **ville ikke komme**. (She didn't want to come / She wouldn't come.)

---

## 5. bør (should / ought to)

**Infinitive**: å burde
**Present**: bør
**Past**: burde

### Present Tense Conjugation:

| Person | Norsk | English |
|--------|-------|---------|
| jeg | bør | I should |
| du | bør | you should |
| han/hun | bør | he/she should |
| vi | bør | we should |
| dere | bør | you should (plural) |
| de | bør | they should |

### When to use:
- ✅ Advice
- ✅ Recommendations
- ✅ What's right/proper
- ✅ Suggestions

### Examples:

**Advice:**
- Du **bør snakke** norsk. (You should speak Norwegian.)
- Jeg **bør gå** nå. (I should go now.)

**Recommendations:**
- Du **bør bo** i Oslo. (You should live in Oslo.)
- Vi **bør gjøre** lekser. (We should do homework.)

**What's proper:**
- Man **bør være** høflig. (One should be polite.)
- Du **bør komme** i tide. (You should come on time.)

### Past tense (burde):

**Past advice/regret:**
- Jeg **burde gå**. (I should have gone / I ought to have gone.)
- Du **burde snakke** norsk. (You should have spoken Norwegian.)

### Negation:
- Du **bør ikke gjøre** det. (You shouldn't do that.)
- Jeg **burde ikke ha kommet**. (I shouldn't have come.)

| Modal | Meaning | Use for | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|---------|

| **bør** | should | advice, recommendations | Du **bør snakke** norsk. |

## Common Confusions

### må ikke vs trenger ikke å

**må ikke** = must not (prohibition)
- Du **må ikke gå**! (You must not go! / Don't go!)

**trenger ikke å** = don't have to (no obligation)
- Du **trenger ikke å gå**. (You don't have to go.)

### skal vs vil (future)

**skal** = planned/scheduled future
- Jeg **skal gå** til skolen i morgen. (I'm going to go to school tomorrow - it's planned)

**vil** = prediction or desire
- Jeg **vil gå** til skolen. (I want to go to school / I will go to school - prediction)

### kan vs får

**kan** = ability/possibility
- Jeg **kan snakke** norsk. (I can speak Norwegian - I'm able to)

**får** = permission (from someone else)
- Jeg **får gå** hjem. (I'm allowed to go home / I get to go home)

## Pattern with Infinitive

Modal verbs are always followed by the **infinitive without "å"**:

✅ Correct:
- Jeg kan **snakke** norsk.
- Du må **gå** nå.
- Vi skal **bo** i Oslo.

❌ Incorrect:
- Jeg kan **å snakke** norsk.
- Du må **å gå** nå.

**Exception**: "trenger" and other non-modal verbs require "å":
- Jeg trenger **å gå**. (I need to go.)

---

## Negation

Add **ikke** after the modal verb:

- Jeg **kan ikke** snakke norsk. (I can't speak Norwegian.)
- Du **må ikke** gå. (You must not go.)
- Vi **skal ikke** komme. (We're not going to come.)
- Hun **vil ikke** gjøre det. (She won't do it / She doesn't want to do it.)
- Jeg **bør ikke** gå. (I shouldn't go.)

---

## Questions with Modal Verbs

Invert the subject and modal verb:

**Statement**: Du kan snakke norsk.
**Question**: **Kan du** snakke norsk?

**Examples:**
- **Kan du** hjelpe meg? (Can you help me?)
- **Må vi** gå nå? (Do we have to go now?)
- **Skal vi** snakke norsk? (Shall we speak Norwegian?)
- **Vil du** ha kaffe? (Do you want coffee?)
- **Bør jeg** gjøre det? (Should I do it?)

---

## Double Modals

You **cannot** use two modal verbs together in Norwegian:

❌ Incorrect:
- Jeg vil kan snakke norsk.

✅ Correct:
- Jeg vil **kunne** snakke norsk. (I want to **be able to** speak Norwegian.)
  - Use infinitive form of second modal!
