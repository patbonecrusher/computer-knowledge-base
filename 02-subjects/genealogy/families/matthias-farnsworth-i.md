# Matthias Farnsworth I

## Personal Information
- **Birth**: c. 1615 (possibly July 20, 1615)
- **Birthplace**: Possibly Eccles, Lancashire, England
- **Death**: January 21, 1689
- **Death Place**: Groton, Middlesex County, Massachusetts
- **Age at Death**: c. 77 years
- **Parents**: Possibly Richard Farnsworth and Elizabeth Marshe/Marsh (not conclusively documented)
- **Occupation**: Weaver

## English Origins

### Possible Lancashire Roots

A Matthias Farnsworth was baptized on **August 20, 1615** in Eccles, Lancashire, England, son of Richard Farnsworth and Elizabeth Marsh. However, **no documentary evidence has been found** definitively linking Matthias Farnsworth, the early settler of Lynn, Massachusetts, to Richard and Elizabeth Farnsworth and to birth in Eccles, England.

The connection is considered likely because:
- The name Matthias was quite unusual in England at that time
- The dates align (born c. 1615)
- Lancashire was a source of many New England immigrants
- But it remains unproven without ship records or other documentation

### Mystery of Immigration

We don't know:
- Exactly when Matthias immigrated to New England
- Which ship he arrived on
- Why he left England
- Whether he came alone or with family

His first three children were likely born in England before immigration, suggesting he came to New England as a married man with a young family, probably in the 1640s-1650s.

## Life in New England

### Lynn, Massachusetts (by 1657)

Matthias Farnsworth was living at **Lynn, Massachusetts** by 1657. Lynn was one of the early settlements of Massachusetts Bay Colony, founded in 1629.

Little is known about his time in Lynn, but he was established there as a weaver and family man.

### Groton, Massachusetts (by 1664)

By 1664, Matthias had moved to **Groton**, a frontier settlement about 35 miles northwest of Boston.

#### Original Proprietor

Matthias Farnsworth was an **original proprietor of Groton**, meaning he was among the founding settlers who received land grants and had voting rights in town affairs. He owned a **twenty-acre right**—a significant land holding.

Being an original proprietor meant:
- He was there at or near the town's founding
- He had status and standing in the community
- He received land grants
- He participated in town governance
- His family would be considered among Groton's founding families

#### Occupation: Weaver

Matthias Farnsworth was a **weaver** in Groton. This was an important trade on the frontier:
- Communities needed cloth for clothing, bedding, household goods
- Weaving required skill and equipment (a loom)
- It provided steady work and income
- It was respectable artisan work

As a weaver, Matthias would have been valued in the community for his essential craft.

### Life on the Massachusetts Frontier

Groton in the 1660s-1680s was a dangerous place:
- On the edge of English settlement
- Vulnerable to attacks during King Philip's War (1675-1678)
- Harsh climate and difficult farming
- Close-knit community dependent on mutual support

Matthias lived through:
- **King Philip's War (1675-1678)**: Devastating conflict between colonists and Native Americans
- **Groton's destruction in 1676**: The town was attacked and burned
- **Rebuilding**: After the war, Groton was gradually resettled

Ten children was a large family, ensuring the Farnsworth name spread widely through Massachusetts and eventually New England.

## Death

Matthias Farnsworth died **January 21, 1689** in Groton, Massachusetts at approximately **77 years of age**—a long life for the colonial period.

He died just a few years after the trauma of King Philip's War and the destruction of Groton. He lived to see the town rebuilt and his children and grandchildren established.

He did not live to see his grandson Matthias III captured in 1704 or transformed into Claude-Mathias Phaneuf—that would happen 15 years after his death.

## Legacy

### Founding Family

Matthias Farnsworth was one of the founding settlers of Groton, Massachusetts. His descendants spread throughout New England and eventually across North America.

The Farnsworth name became prominent in Groton and the surrounding area, with multiple generations remaining in the region.

### The Phaneuf Connection

Through his son Matthias Jr. and grandson Matthias III (who became Claude-Mathias Phaneuf), the English weaver from Lancashire became the great-grandfather of **all Phaneufs in North America**.

This creates an interesting genealogical connection:
- **English origins** through Matthias I
- **New England Puritan** culture through Matthias II
- **French-Canadian Catholic** transformation through Matthias III/Claude-Mathias

### Descendants

Matthias Farnsworth's numerous descendants include:
- Multiple Farnsworth lines throughout New England
- The entire Phaneuf family (through grandson Claude-Mathias)
- Connections to many other New England families through intermarriage

## Historical Context

### The Great Migration (1620s-1640s)

Matthias likely immigrated during or shortly after the **Great Migration**, when approximately 20,000 Puritans left England for New England between 1620 and 1640.

Reasons for migration included:
- Religious persecution of Puritans in England
- Economic opportunity
- Desire for land ownership
- Escape from political turmoil

### Lancashire, England

If Matthias was indeed from Eccles, Lancashire:
- Lancashire was in northwest England
- Eccles was near Manchester
- The area had strong Puritan sympathies
- Many Lancashire residents immigrated to New England

### Groton's Founding and Destruction

- **Founded**: 1655 (Matthias arrived by 1664)
- **Destroyed**: March 1676 during King Philip's War
- **Resettled**: Gradually after 1676
- **Matthias's role**: Original proprietor and rebuilder

## Genealogical Notes

### Challenges in Research

Researching Matthias Farnsworth I presents challenges:
- No proven ship records linking him to England
- No definitive proof of his English parentage
- Limited records from early Lynn and Groton
- Many records lost in King Philip's War destruction

### What We Know for Certain

- He was in Lynn, MA by 1657
- He was in Groton, MA by 1664
- He was an original proprietor of Groton
- He was a weaver
- He married Mary Farr
- He had ten children, including Matthias Jr.
- He died January 21, 1689 at about age 77

### The Name Connection

The unusual name "Matthias" helps with genealogical research:
- Not a common name in England or New England
- Makes it easier to track individuals
- The Eccles baptismal record is compelling evidence of English origins
- But without ship records, the connection remains probable but not proven
