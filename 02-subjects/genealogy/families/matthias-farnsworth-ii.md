# Matthias Farnsworth II (Jr.)

## Personal Information
- **Birth**: c. 1649
- **Birthplace**: Likely England (before family's immigration) or Lynn, Massachusetts
- **Death**: 1693 (during the Indian wars)
- **Death Place**: Groton, Middlesex County, Massachusetts
- **Parents**: Matthias Farnsworth I and Mary Farr
- **Occupation**: Farmer, militia member

## Early Life

### Childhood in England or Early Massachusetts

Matthias Farnsworth Jr. was born around 1649, making him one of the first three children of Matthias Farnsworth I and Mary Farr, likely born in England before the family's immigration to New England.

If born in England (most likely):
- He would have been an infant or young child during the voyage to America
- His earliest memories would be of New England, not England
- He grew up fully as a New England colonist

If born in Lynn, Massachusetts shortly after arrival:
- He would be a first-generation American
- Entirely formed by the New England Puritan experience

### Growing Up on the Frontier

Matthias Jr. grew up in:
- **Lynn, Massachusetts** (until he was about 15, c. 1664)
- **Groton, Massachusetts** (from c. 1664 onward)

As the son of a weaver and original proprietor, he grew up with:
- Puritan religious training (church, Bible study, Sabbath observance)
- Frontier skills (farming, hunting, building)
- Community responsibilities (militia training from adolescence)
- The constant awareness of danger from the wilderness and potential conflict

### King Philip's War (1675-1678)

When Matthias Jr. was in his mid-twenties (around 26-29 years old), **King Philip's War** devastated New England.

For Groton specifically:
- **March 1676**: Groton was attacked and burned
- Matthias Jr. would have fought to defend the town
- Many residents fled to safer towns
- The Farnsworth garrison (possibly his father's property) may have been established during this period

This war shaped Matthias Jr.'s entire adult life—he grew up in relative peace but came of age in brutal warfare.

## Marriage and Family

### Marriage to Sarah Nutting

**Date**: March 1, 1681 in Groton, Middlesex, Massachusetts

**Wife**: Sarah Nutting
- **Born**: March 29, 1663 in Groton
- **Parents**: John Nutting and Sarah Eggleston
- **Age at Marriage**: 17 years old (Matthias was about 32)

Sarah was significantly younger than Matthias—about 15 years. This age gap was not unusual for the time, especially for a man marrying after years of war and instability.

### The Nutting Family

Sarah's father, John Nutting, was also a Groton settler. The marriage connected two founding families of Groton.

### Children

Matthias and Sarah had at least six children:

1. **Joseph Farnsworth** - born January 7, 1682, Groton
2. **Ebenezer Farnsworth** - birth date unknown
3. **[Name unknown]** - possibly others
4. **Josiah Farnsworth** - born February 24, 1687, Groton
5. **[Name unknown]** - possibly others
6. **Matthias Farnsworth III** - born August 6, 1690, Groton
   - Later became Claude-Mathias Phaneuf after capture by Abenaki in 1704

Six children in less than 10 years of marriage—a common pattern for colonial families.

## Life in Groton

### Farmer and Militia Member

Matthias Jr. worked as a **farmer**, cultivating land in Groton. Like most frontier settlers, farming was:
- Hard physical labor clearing land, planting, harvesting
- Subsistence agriculture (feeding the family first, selling any surplus)
- Supplemented by hunting, gathering, trading
- A year-round cycle of work

### The Farnsworth Garrison

On **March 17, 1692**, Matthias Farnsworth was **assigned to the Farnsworth garrison** in Groton, organized for defense against Indian raids.

A **garrison** was a fortified house where families could take shelter during attacks. The fact that it bore the Farnsworth name suggests it was likely:
- His father's property (Matthias I died in 1689, so this may have been inherited)
- A substantial, defensible structure
- A responsibility passed to Matthias Jr. as head of the family

Being assigned to a garrison meant:
- Military responsibility for the area's defense
- Providing shelter for neighbors during attacks
- Maintaining weapons and fortifications
- Standing watch rotation
- Responding to alarms

## Death in the Indian Wars

### The Ongoing Conflict

While King Philip's War ended in 1678, conflict between English settlers and Native American nations continued through the 1680s and 1690s.

By the early 1690s, **King William's War** (1688-1697) brought renewed violence to the frontier:
- French-allied Native Americans raided English settlements
- Groton remained vulnerable
- Garrison houses were essential for survival

### Matthias's Death (1693)

Matthias Farnsworth Jr. **probably died during the Indian wars** in 1693.

An **inventory of his estate** was taken by his widow Sarah on **November 8, 1693**.

We don't know exactly how he died:
- Killed in a raid on Groton?
- Killed while defending another settlement?
- Died in an ambush while working fields?
- Killed in a retaliatory expedition?

But the timing (during active warfare) and the context (his garrison assignment) strongly suggest he died in combat or in a raid.

### The Widow and Children

Matthias's death left:
- **Sarah**, a 30-year-old widow
- **Joseph**, age 11
- **Ebenezer**, age unknown
- **Josiah**, age 6
- **Matthias III**, age 3
- **Possibly other children**

Sarah had to:
- Manage the estate
- Raise six children alone
- Survive on a dangerous frontier
- Make impossible decisions about the family's future

This was the brutal reality of frontier life—sudden death, widows left with young children, survival always in question.

### Sarah's Remarriage

On **December 16, 1698** in Concord, Massachusetts, Sarah married **John Stone**.

By remarrying after five years, Sarah:
- Provided security for her children
- Gained help running the farm
- Followed a common pattern (widows usually remarried within a few years)

But this meant young Matthias III grew up with:
- No memory of his biological father (he was only 3 when Matthias died)
- A stepfather (John Stone)
- The trauma of frontier warfare all around him
- The knowledge that his father had died defending their community

Is it any wonder that when 14-year-old Matthias was captured in 1704, he eventually chose to stay in New France rather than return to Massachusetts? What did Massachusetts hold for him but memories of death and war?

## Legacy

### A Life Cut Short

Matthias Farnsworth Jr. lived only about 44 years. He:
- Survived King Philip's War in his twenties
- Married and had six children in his thirties
- Died in combat in his early forties, leaving young children

He never knew that his youngest son would be captured, transformed, and become the progenitor of an entirely new family name in a different culture.

### Father of Claude-Mathias Phaneuf

Through his youngest son, Matthias III/Claude-Mathias, Matthias Farnsworth Jr. became the father of the **founder of all Phaneufs in North America**.

The English farmer and militia member who died defending Groton became, through his son's capture and transformation, a link between:
- English Puritan New England
- French Catholic New France
- The Farnsworth and Phaneuf family lines

### The Tragic Pattern

Matthias Jr.'s death in 1693 during the Indian wars foreshadowed his son's capture in 1704 during the same ongoing conflicts. The colonial wars that killed the father also took the son—but in a different way.

- **Father**: Died fighting
- **Son**: Captured, transformed, assimilated

Both were casualties of the same brutal colonial conflicts.

## Historical Context

### King William's War (1688-1697)

The conflict that likely killed Matthias was King William's War:
- Part of the larger War of the Grand Alliance in Europe
- In North America: French and English colonial forces, each with Indigenous allies, fought for control
- Characterized by raids and counter-raids on frontier settlements
- Groton and other Massachusetts frontier towns were vulnerable targets
- Many settlers killed, captured, or forced to abandon their homes

### Garrison Houses

Garrison houses like the Farnsworth garrison were critical for frontier defense:
- Fortified structures with thick walls
- Gun ports for defensive fire
- Space for multiple families to shelter during attacks
- Stocked with weapons, ammunition, food
- Men assigned to defend and maintain them

Being assigned to a garrison was both an honor (trusted with community defense) and a burden (constant danger and responsibility).

### The Frontier Experience

Life on the Massachusetts frontier in the 1680s-1690s meant:
- Constant vigilance against attack
- Militia service required of all able men
- Crop failures could mean starvation
- Disease was rampant
- Death was commonplace (war, childbirth, illness, accidents)
- Community mutual support was essential for survival

Matthias Jr. lived this reality every day of his adult life.

## Genealogical Notes

### What We Know

- Born c. 1649 (possibly in England)
- Married Sarah Nutting, March 1, 1681
- At least six children, including Matthias III/Claude-Mathias
- Assigned to Farnsworth garrison, March 17, 1692
- Died 1693 (inventory November 8, 1693)
- Widow remarried John Stone, December 16, 1698

### What Remains Unknown

- Exact birth date and place
- Details of his military service during King Philip's War
- Exactly how he died in 1693
- Where he is buried (likely Groton, but no marker survives)
- What he thought about raising a family on such a dangerous frontier
- Whether he had any premonition of the violence that would claim him and later capture his youngest son

### Research Challenges

- Many Groton records destroyed in the 1676 attack
- Militia records incomplete
- No detailed account of his death
- Common name (Matthias Farnsworth) makes distinguishing individuals difficult

## Connection to Claude-Mathias Phaneuf

Matthias Farnsworth Jr. died when his youngest son was only three years old. He never knew that:

- His son would be captured at age 14 by Abenaki warriors
- His son would be ransomed by a French priest
- His son would convert to Catholicism
- His son would take a French name
- His son would marry a French woman
- His son would become the ancestor of thousands of French-Canadians

If Matthias Jr. had lived, would things have been different? Would a living father have fought harder to ransom Matthias III back from captivity? Would the boy have been more determined to escape and return home?

We'll never know. But Matthias Jr.'s death in 1693 shaped the conditions that led to his son's transformation into Claude-Mathias Phaneuf.
