# Louis Badaillac dit Laplante

## Personal Information
- **Birth**: c. 1644
- **Birthplace**: Diocese of Périgueux, Périgord (Dordogne), France
- **Death**: Before 1705 (died before daughter Therese's marriage in 1705)
- **Also known as**: Louis Badaillac dit Laplante, Louis Badayac

## Immigration to New France

### Military Service
Louis Badaillac arrived in Canada as a soldier in the **Carignan-Salières Regiment**, Froment Company.

### Ship Voyage - Le Vieux Simeon
- **Ship Name**: Le Vieux Simeon (Vieux Siméon de Dunkerdam)
- **Ship Type**: Chartered Dutch ship
- **Commander**: Captain Pierre-André de Froment
- **Departure**: La Rochelle, France - April 19, 1665
- **Arrival**: Quebec City - June 19, 1665
- **Voyage Duration**: Approximately 2 months

### Confirmation
Louis Badaillac, de l'évêché de Périgueux, was confirmed on May 20, 1669 at Fort Saint-Louis de Chambly.

## Historical Context

### The Carignan-Salières Regiment
- About 1,200-1,300 soldiers and 80 officers arrived in Quebec in summer/fall 1665
- Mission: Protect inhabitants and eliminate the Iroquois threat
- The regiment constructed fortifications along the Richelieu River
- Initial deployment to Trois-Rivières for winter, then charged with construction of Fort Saint-Louis (later known as Chambly)
- Peace declared between French and Five Nations Iroquois on July 10, 1667 (lasted 18 years)
- Regiment returned to France in 1668, but roughly 400-450 soldiers remained as settlers

### Périgueux in the 1640s-1650s
- The city had experienced the Fronde (civil disturbances, 1648-1653)
- Louis XIV granted amnesty to Périgueux in 1654 for its role in the Fronde
- After the amnesty, Périgueux entered an era of peace and stability
- This followed centuries of turmoil from the Hundred Years' War and Wars of Religion
- The 17th century marked a period of rebuilding for the city

## Family

### Marriage
- **Spouse**: Catherine Lalore (also spelled de Lalore)
- **Marriage**: c. 1672 in Sorel
- **Note**: No marriage contract or marriage record survives

### Children

Louis and Catherine had eight known children (original spelling preserved):

1. **Marie Catherine Badaillac** - baptized June 22, 1673, Sorel
   - Married Pierre Lamy in 1689

2. **Marie Badaillac** - born July 27, 1675, baptized July 28, Sorel
   - Also recorded as Marie Madeleine Badailla
   - Married Bernard Delomé on June 14, 1694, St-Laurent IO

3. **Anne Élisabeth Badaillon** (sic) - born October 14, 1677, baptized October 24, Sorel
   - Died young: buried February 28, 1690, Sorel

4. **Louis Badailla** - born March 26, 1680, baptized April 4, Sorel
   - Engaged as voyageur to Detroit from May 27, 1701 to July 10, 1703
   - Part of the Cadillac convoy that founded Detroit in 1701

5. **Gilles Badailla** - born May 3, 1682, baptized May 4, Sorel
   - Also known as Gilles Badaillac dit Laplante
   - First marriage: Françoise Giguière on February 24, 1705, St-François-du-Lac
   - Second marriage: Marguerite Modoue on November 23, 1722, St-François-du-Lac (no mention of previous marriage)
   - Third marriage: Marie Claude Minville on July 22, 1725, St-François-du-Lac (recorded as widower; notice of consanguinity as cousins removed from germain in 3rd degree)

6. **Thérèse Badaillac** - baptized August 21, 1685, Sorel
   - Married Pierre Guignard on January 4, 1705, Repentigny

7. **Claire** - born around 1686
   - Married François Lancteau on May 9, 1707, Sorel (at age 21)

8. **Catherine Badaillac** - baptized July 4, 1690, Sorel
   - Also recorded as Marie Catherine Bataillac
   - Married Bonaventure Compain dit L'Espérance on June 10, 1706, Montreal (Notre-Dame) at age 16

### Death
Both Louis Badaillac and Catherine de Lalore died before their daughter Therese's marriage in 1705.

## Genealogical Notes
- No marriage record or contract survives to give us his parents' names
- While some genealogical sources list parents' names, these remain unsourced and unverified
- His actual parents remain unknown

## Detroit Connection

Louis Badaillac's son, also named Louis, was part of one of the most important expeditions in North American history: the founding of Detroit.

### The Cadillac Convoy (1701)
- Antoine de la Mothe Cadillac departed Montreal on June 5, 1701 with 100 people (50 colonists, 50 soldiers) in 25 canoes
- Arrived July 24, 1701, establishing Fort Pontchartrain du Détroit (Detroit)
- Young Louis Badaillac (age 21) was among the original settlers
- He served as a voyageur from May 27, 1701 to July 10, 1703

This makes Louis Badaillac (father) not only a Carignan-Salières soldier who helped secure New France from the Iroquois, but also the father of one of Detroit's founding settlers.
