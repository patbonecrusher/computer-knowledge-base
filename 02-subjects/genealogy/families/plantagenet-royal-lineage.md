# Plantagenet Royal Lineage Through Wolcott-Cornwall Line

## Summary

Through **Matilda Cornwall** (c.1388-1468), who married into the Wolcott family, our lineage connects to the **House of Plantagenet**, specifically to **Henry II Plantagenet** (1133-1189), the first Plantagenet King of England.

This connection runs through 29 generations and involves one instance of illegitimacy (Richard Plantagenet of Cornwall, c.1252-1296, illegitimate son of Richard, 1st Earl of Cornwall).

## Key Individuals

### Matilda Cornwall (c.1388-1468) - Gateway Ancestor

**Personal Information:**
- **Birth**: c. 1388, Walcot, Shropshire, England
- **Death**: c. 1468
- **Marriage**: c. 1416 to John Wolcott (or to father of Roger Wolcott)
- **Children**: Roger Wolcott (1425-1469) and possibly others

**Significance:**
- **Gateway Ancestor**: Person through whom millions of modern descendants can claim Plantagenet ancestry
- Connected the royal Cornwall line to the common Wolcott family
- Brought Plantagenet blood into the Wolcott lineage

**Status:**
- Daughter of gentry family (Cornwalls of Burford, Shropshire)
- Not nobility, but from established landed family
- Descended from illegitimate royal line (Richard of Cornwall)

### Richard Plantagenet of Cornwall (c.1252-1296)

**Personal Information:**
- **Birth**: c. 1252
- **Death**: 1296, killed at Siege of Berwick (Scottish Wars)
- **Father**: Richard, 1st Earl of Cornwall (1209-1272)
- **Mother**: Joan de Valletort
- **Status**: Illegitimate but acknowledged

**Life:**
- **Manor of Thunnock, Lincolnshire**: Granted to him in 1283 by his half-brother, the 2nd Earl of Cornwall
- **Founder**: Established the Cornwall family of Burford, Shropshire
- **Styled**: Sometimes called "Barons of Burford" though not formal peers
- **Death**: Killed at the Siege of Berwick in 1296 during Edward I's Scottish campaigns

**Significance:**
- **The one illegitimacy** in the otherwise legitimate Plantagenet descent
- Acknowledged by his royal father
- Founded gentry family that preserved Plantagenet blood
- Walter Lee Sheppard Jr. noted: "through the family of Cornwall, uninterrupted male descent from the Plantagenets, with but this one illegitimacy, still exists"

### Richard, 1st Earl of Cornwall (1209-1272)

**Personal Information:**
- **Birth**: January 5, 1209, Winchester Castle, England
- **Death**: April 2, 1272, Berkhamsted Castle, England
- **Burial**: Hailes Abbey (which he founded)
- **Parents**: King John and Isabella of Angoulême
- **Siblings**: Henry III (King), Joan (Queen of Scotland), Isabella (Holy Roman Empress), Eleanor

**Titles:**
- **Earl of Cornwall** (from 1225)
- **Count of Poitou** (1225-1243)
- **King of Germany** (elected 1257, though never crowned Holy Roman Emperor)

**Marriages:**
1. Isabel Marshal (1231-1240) - daughter of Earl of Pembroke
2. Sanchia of Provence (1243-1261) - sister of Queen of France
3. Beatrice of Falkenburg (1269-1272)

**Illegitimate Children:**
- **Richard of Cornwall** (c.1252-1296) - our ancestor - mother: Joan de Valletort
- Possibly others not well-documented

**Achievements:**
- One of the wealthiest men in Europe (controlled tin mines of Cornwall)
- Led crusade to Holy Land (1240-1241)
- Elected King of Germany (essentially bought votes with his wealth)
- Founded Hailes Abbey
- Fought in Barons' Wars supporting his brother Henry III

**Legacy:**
- Through his illegitimate son Richard, his bloodline spread to millions of modern descendants
- His legitimate children either died without issue or their lines died out
- The illegitimate Cornwall line became his lasting genetic legacy

### King John of England (c.1166-1216)

**Personal Information:**
- **Birth**: December 24, 1166, Beaumont Palace, Oxford, England
- **Death**: October 18-19, 1216, Newark Castle, England (possibly from dysentery)
- **Burial**: Worcester Cathedral
- **Reign**: 1199-1216 (17 years)
- **Parents**: Henry II and Eleanor of Aquitaine
- **Nickname**: "Lackland" (youngest son with little inheritance)

**Marriages:**
1. Isabella of Gloucester (1189-1199, annulled - no children)
2. Isabella of Angoulême (1200-1216)

**Children with Isabella of Angoulême:**
- **Henry III** (1207-1272) - became King of England
- **Richard, Earl of Cornwall** (1209-1272) - our ancestor
- **Joan** (1210-1238) - married Alexander II of Scotland
- **Isabella** (1214-1241) - married Holy Roman Emperor Frederick II
- **Eleanor** (1215-1275) - married Simon de Montfort

**Major Events of Reign:**
- **Loss of Normandy** (1204) - lost most English possessions in France
- **Interdict** (1208-1213) - England placed under papal interdict; John excommunicated
- **Magna Carta** (June 15, 1215) - forced to sign by rebelling barons
- **First Barons' War** (1215-1217) - civil war following Magna Carta
- **Death during civil war** (1216)

**Historical Reputation:**
- "Bad King John" in popular culture (villain of Robin Hood legends)
- Considered one of England's worst kings by traditional historians
- Modern historians more nuanced: effective administrator, but political failures
- Magna Carta became foundation of constitutional government despite John's intentions

**Significance for Our Lineage:**
- Father of Richard, 1st Earl of Cornwall
- Grandfather (through illegitimate line) of the Cornwall family
- Son of Henry II Plantagenet, founder of the dynasty

### Henry II Plantagenet (1133-1189)

**Personal Information:**
- **Birth**: March 5, 1133, Le Mans, France
- **Death**: July 6, 1189, Chinon, France
- **Burial**: Fontevraud Abbey (Plantagenet mausoleum)
- **Reign**: 1154-1189 (35 years)
- **Parents**: Geoffrey Plantagenet, Count of Anjou and Empress Matilda (daughter of Henry I of England)

**Marriage:**
- **Eleanor of Aquitaine** (married 1152)
- Former Queen of France (annulled from Louis VII)
- One of the most powerful women in medieval Europe
- Brought Duchy of Aquitaine to Henry

**Children (with Eleanor):**
1. William IX, Count of Poitiers (1153-1156) - died young
2. Henry the Young King (1155-1183) - crowned as heir but rebelled; died before inheriting
3. Matilda, Duchess of Saxony (1156-1189)
4. **Richard I "the Lionheart"** (1157-1199) - became King 1189
5. Geoffrey II, Duke of Brittany (1158-1186)
6. Eleanor, Queen of Castile (1161-1214)
7. Joan, Queen of Sicily (1165-1199)
8. **John "Lackland"** (c.1166-1216) - **our ancestor** - became King 1199

**Plus illegitimate children:**
- Geoffrey, Archbishop of York (c.1152-1212)
- William Longespée, 3rd Earl of Salisbury (c.1176-1226)

**The Angevin Empire:**
Henry controlled an empire stretching from Scotland to the Pyrenees:
- **England** - inherited through mother
- **Normandy** - inherited through mother
- **Anjou, Maine, Touraine** - inherited from father
- **Aquitaine** - through marriage to Eleanor
- **Overlordship**: Wales, Scotland, Brittany, Ireland

**Major Achievements:**
- **Legal reforms**: Foundation of English common law
- **Administrative reforms**: Efficient royal bureaucracy
- **Conquest of Ireland** (1171)
- **Strengthened royal authority** over feudal lords
- **Pipe Rolls**: Detailed financial records

**Conflicts:**
- **Thomas Becket affair** (1162-1170) - Archbishop murdered in Canterbury Cathedral
- **Rebellions of his sons** (1173-1174, 1183, 1189)
- **Eleanor's imprisonment** (1173-1189) for supporting sons' rebellions
- **War with Philip II of France**

**Death:**
- Defeated by his sons Richard and John (allied with Philip II of France)
- Died broken-hearted after learning John betrayed him
- Last words allegedly: "Shame, shame on a conquered king"

**Legacy:**
- **Founded Plantagenet dynasty** (ruled England 1154-1485, 331 years)
- **Legal system**: Foundation of English common law still used today
- **Empire builder**: Created the Angevin Empire
- **Tragic family**: Sons rebelled repeatedly; dynasty ended in family conflict
- **Genetic legacy**: Through many children (legitimate and illegitimate), millions descend from him

**Significance for Our Lineage:**
- Great-great-grandfather of Richard of Cornwall (our direct ancestor)
- Founder of the Plantagenet dynasty
- Through him, we connect to William the Conqueror and other Norman/French royalty
- Represents the pinnacle of our royal ancestry

## The Cornwall Family Line

### Overview

The **Cornwall family of Burford, Shropshire** descended from Richard Plantagenet of Cornwall (c.1252-1296), illegitimate son of Richard, 1st Earl of Cornwall.

**Generations:**
1. Richard Plantagenet of Cornwall (c.1252-1296)
2. Geoffrey Cornwall (c.1288-c.1335)
3. Richard Cornwall (1311-1343)
4. Geoffrey Cornwall (c.1335-1365)
5. Richard Cornwall (1359-1443)
6. Matilda Cornwall (c.1388-1468) → married into Wolcott family

**Territory:**
- **Burford, Shropshire** (main seat)
- **Berington, Herefordshire** (later seat)
- **Manor of Thunnock, Lincolnshire** (original grant to Richard in 1283)

**Status:**
- Sometimes styled "Barons of Burford" but not formal peers
- Gentry family with royal descent
- Maintained lands for several generations
- By Matilda's time (late 1300s), established but not wealthy nobility

**Significance:**
- Preserved Plantagenet bloodline through illegitimate line
- Matilda Cornwall became gateway ancestor for millions
- Intermarried with other gentry families (including Wolcott)

## The Wolcott Family Line

### English Wolcotts (c.1500-1634)

**Location**: Tolland and Axbridge, Somerset, England

1. **Thomas Wolcott** (c.1500-c.1554) - Of Tolland, Somerset
2. **Thomas Wolcott Jr.** (c.1525-c.1572) - Of Tolland
3. **John Wolcott** (c.1545-1589) - Of Tolland
4. **Edward Wolcott** (c.1570-1640) - Mercer and Mayor of Axbridge, Somerset
5. **John Wolcott I** (before 1599-1638) - Baptized Axbridge, May 13, 1599

**Occupations:**
- Merchants, craftsmen, local officials
- Edward was a "mercer" (cloth merchant) and served as Mayor of Axbridge
- Respectable middle-class/gentry status

### American Wolcotts (1634-present)

**Immigration:**
- **John Wolcott I** immigrated on ship _Recovery_ in 1634
- Sailed from Weymouth, Dorset, England
- Listed as "John Woolcocke" on passenger list
- Settled first in Salem, then Cambridge (1636), then Watertown (1637), Massachusetts

**The Missing Link:**
- **John Wolcott I** (before 1599-1638) → **John Wolcott II** (c.1634-1690)
- Originally uncertain due to lack of direct documentary evidence
- **DNA evidence** (Y22158 haplogroup) confirmed connection in early 2000s
- Wolcott DNA Project provided definitive proof

**Subsequent Generations:**
1. John Wolcott II (c.1634-1690) - Newbury, Massachusetts
2. John Wolcott III (1660-after 1749)
3. John Wolcott IV (1696-c.1728)
4. John Wolcott V(2) (c.1730-after 1778)
5. **Abner Ebenezer Wolcott** (1747-1833) - United Empire Loyalist, fled to Canada 1778

**Characteristics:**
- Many "Johns" in succession (naming pattern)
- Farmers, craftsmen, respectable colonists
- By Abner's time, established American family
- Split between American Patriots and Loyalists during Revolution

## DNA Evidence: The Wolcott DNA Project

### Project Overview

**Founded**: 2004 by Charles W. Wolcott and John B. Wolcott

**Purpose:**
- Establish DNA base data for Wolcott/Woolcott/Walcott/Wilcott/Woollacott families
- Identify common ancestors
- Understand DNA mutations
- Prove or disprove conjectural relationships

### Key Discovery: Y22158 Haplogroup

**Finding:**
- Descendants of John Wolcott of Watertown belong to **haplogroup Y22158**
- This haplogroup branched from Y20600 around **1570 AD**
- Henry Wolcott descendants (different line, immigrated to Connecticut 1630) belong to Y20600
- **No other Wolcott branches** belong to Y22158

**Significance:**
- Provides **definitive proof** of descent from John Wolcott I of Watertown
- Confirms John Wolcott II was son of John Wolcott I
- Confirms all later Wolcotts descend from John I (including Abner, Silas, Brookfield community)

**Specific Evidence:**
- Gary Lynn Wolcott's positive test for SNP Y22158
- Multiple tested descendants all show Y22158
- Pattern matches expected mutations from 1570s origin

### What This Proves

✓ **Abner Wolcott** → descended from John Wolcott I of Watertown
✓ **John Wolcott I** → descended from Edward Wolcott of Axbridge
✓ **Edward Wolcott** → descended from earlier Wolcotts of Somerset
✓ **Wolcott line** → connects to Matilda Cornwall and Plantagenet royalty

## Historical Context

### Gateway Ancestors and Royal Descent

**What is a Gateway Ancestor?**
- Person with proven royal lineage who has many commoner descendants
- Creates pathway for millions to claim royal ancestry
- Usually involves:
  - Illegitimate children of nobility (like Richard of Cornwall)
  - Younger sons and daughters who married below their rank
  - Gentry families that became common over generations

**Why Matilda Cornwall is a Gateway:**
1. Descended from Plantagenet royalty (through illegitimate line)
2. Married into Wolcott family (gentry/common)
3. Numerous descendants through Wolcott line
4. Wolcotts immigrated to America, spreading lineage widely

**How Many Descend from Henry II?**
- Estimated millions of people with European ancestry
- Exponential growth of descendants over 29 generations
- Pedigree collapse (cousins marrying) means actual number lower than mathematical maximum
- But still: millions can trace descent from medieval royalty

### The Significance (and Insignificance) of Royal Descent

**What it means:**
✓ Documented lineage back 850+ years to medieval kings
✓ Connection to some of history's most significant figures
✓ Understanding of how social classes mixed over time
✓ Part of the complex web of European genealogy

**What it doesn't mean:**
✗ No special rights, titles, or claims
✗ Not unique (millions share this ancestry)
✗ Doesn't confer nobility or special status
✗ "Royal blood" is diluted to insignificance over 29 generations

**Practical Use:**
- Genealogical interest and research
- Connection to historical events and figures
- Understanding family history and identity
- Appreciation for human migration and adaptation

## Controversies and Uncertainties

### John I to John II Connection (RESOLVED)

**The Problem:**
- No direct documentary evidence connecting John Wolcott I (1599-1638) to John Wolcott II (c.1634-1690)
- Based on circumstantial evidence: timing, location, property ownership, family connections

**The Solution:**
- **Wolcott DNA Project** (2004) used Y-DNA testing
- Discovered Y22158 haplogroup unique to John I's descendants
- All tested descendants of John II also carry Y22158
- **Definitive proof** of father-son relationship

**Current Status**: ✓ RESOLVED - DNA confirms connection

### Matilda Cornwall's Marriage

**Uncertainty:**
- Some sources say Matilda married John Wolcott
- Others say she married the father of Roger Wolcott
- Exact relationship unclear

**What is certain:**
- Matilda Cornwall (c.1388-1468) is Roger Wolcott's (1425-1469) mother
- Roger Wolcott carried both Wolcott name and Cornwall/Plantagenet blood
- Line continues through Roger to modern descendants

**Current Status**: Minor uncertainty about exact marriage, but maternal line confirmed

### Cornwall Family Details

**Challenges:**
- Medieval records for gentry families less complete than for royalty
- Some generational connections based on property records and family histories
- A few gaps in exact dates and relationships

**What is well-documented:**
- Richard Plantagenet of Cornwall (c.1252-1296) was illegitimate son of Earl Richard
- He founded Cornwall family of Burford/Berington
- Matilda Cornwall (c.1388-1468) descended from that line
- Basic generational sequence is sound

**Current Status**: Well-supported by available evidence, though not as detailed as royal records
