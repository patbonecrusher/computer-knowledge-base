# Claude-Mathias Phaneuf (born Matthias Farnsworth III)

## Personal Information
- **Birth Name**: Matthias Farnsworth III
- **French Name**: Claude-Mathias Fanef/Phaneuf
- **Birth**: August 6, 1690
- **Birthplace**: Groton, Middlesex County, Massachusetts (English colony)
- **Death**: August 8, 1773
- **Death Place**: St-Antoine-sur-Richelieu, Quebec
- **Parents**: Matthias Farnsworth II and Sarah Nutting (English Puritans)

## The Captivity Story

### The Raid on Groton (1704)

On **August 1, 1704** (old English calendar) or **August 11, 1704** (French Gregorian calendar), 14-year-old Matthias Farnsworth III was captured during an Abenaki raid on Groton, Massachusetts, during **Queen Anne's War** (1702-1713).

- **Context**: Colonial conflicts between French and English colonies
- **Captors**: Approximately 20 Abenaki warriors allied with the French
- **Circumstances**: Young Matthias and other men were harvesting crops in a field when captured
- **Destination**: Forced march to Montreal

### Life Among the Abenaki

Matthias remained a captive of the Indigenous peoples from August 1704 until late 1705 or early 1706. He lived at the **Sault-au-Récollet mission** near Montreal, among Indigenous groups and French missionaries.

This was a common practice during the colonial wars - Indigenous allies of the French would raid English settlements and bring captives back to New France, where they could be:
- Ransomed back to English colonies
- Adopted into Indigenous communities
- Sold to French colonists
- Converted to Catholicism and integrated into French society

### Redemption and Conversion

Around **1705-1706**, Matthias was ransomed by **François Vachon de Belmont**, the Sulpician director and Lord of the Island of Montreal, who "redeemed him from the Indians who had him in slavery."

**Baptism**: January 10, 1706, at Notre-Dame Basilica, Montreal
- **Name**: Mathias-Claude Farneth (first French version)
- **Godfather**: Claude de Ramezay (Governor of Montreal)
- **Godmother**: Elisabeth Souart, wife of Charles le Moyne de Longueuil, Baron de Longueuil

These were extremely prominent godparents - the Governor and the wife of a Baron. This suggests François Vachon de Belmont's high regard for the young convert and perhaps hope he would become a respected member of French society.

## Transformation to French Identity

### Name Evolution

The English surname "Farnsworth" went through multiple transformations over 50 years:

1. **Farnsworth** (English original)
2. **Farneth** (1706 baptism)
3. **Farnouth**
4. **Fanef** (1713 marriage contract)
5. **Faneuf**
6. **Faneuffe**
7. **Phaneffe**
8. **Phaneuf** (standardized by mid-1800s)

The Quebec French pronunciation /fanœf/ emphasized nasal vowels and simplified consonants, eventually leading to the phonetic spelling "Phaneuf."

### Integration into New France

Unlike many captives who were eventually redeemed and returned to New England, Matthias chose to remain in New France. He:
- Converted to Catholicism
- Adopted French language and customs
- Changed his name to Claude-Mathias
- Married into French-Canadian society
- Never returned to Massachusetts

## Marriage and Family

### Marriage
- **Date**: September 1713 (contract signed)
- **Spouse**: Marie Catherine Charpentier (daughter Lapaille)
- **Location**: His neighbor's daughter in Montreal area
- **Age at Marriage**: 23 years old

### Children

Claude-Mathias and Catherine had **12 children**, establishing the Phaneuf line in North America.

**All Phaneufs worldwide descend from this union** - making Claude-Mathias the sole progenitor of the Phaneuf surname in North America.

## Historical Significance

### New England Captive Narrative

Claude-Mathias Phaneuf represents a well-documented phenomenon in colonial North American history: the "New England captive." During the colonial wars between French and English colonies (particularly King William's War, Queen Anne's War, and King George's War), thousands of English colonists were captured by French-allied Indigenous peoples.

Many captives:
- Were eventually ransomed and returned
- But some, especially children and young adults, assimilated into French or Indigenous society
- Converted to Catholicism
- Married and stayed in New France

Famous examples include Eunice Williams (captured 1704, became Aongote) and many others.

### Unique Aspect

What makes Claude-Mathias unique is that:
- He is the **sole ancestor of ALL Phaneufs** in North America
- His story is well-documented
- His prominent godparents suggest his integration into the elite of French colonial society
- The name transformation from Farnsworth to Phaneuf is clearly traceable

## Historical Context

### Queen Anne's War (1702-1713)

- Second of the French and Indian Wars
- Part of the larger War of Spanish Succession in Europe
- Involved raids on English settlements by French and their Indigenous allies
- The 1704 raid on Groton was one of many such raids
- Most famous raid: Deerfield Massacre (February 1704), just months before Matthias's capture

### The Abenaki

The Abenaki were Indigenous peoples of what is now Vermont, New Hampshire, and Maine. During the colonial wars, they were allied with the French against English expansion. They conducted numerous raids into New England, bringing captives back to missions near Montreal.

### Sault-au-Récollet Mission

A Catholic mission near Montreal where Indigenous converts and captives lived. It was part of the French strategy to:
- Convert Indigenous peoples
- Create buffer communities between French settlements and English colonies
- Integrate English captives into French society

## Death

Claude-Mathias Phaneuf died on **August 8, 1773** in **St-Antoine-sur-Richelieu, Quebec**, at the age of 83. He had lived 69 years in Quebec - far longer than the 14 years he spent in Massachusetts.

## Legacy

### The Phaneuf Family

From Claude-Mathias and Catherine's 12 children descended all the Phaneuf families in:
- Quebec
- The United States
- Worldwide French-Canadian diaspora

### A Bridge Between Two Worlds

Claude-Mathias represents:
- The violence and tragedy of colonial conflicts
- The complex relationships between English, French, and Indigenous peoples
- The fluidity of identity in colonial North America
- The assimilation of English captives into French society
- A unique link between New England Puritan heritage and French-Canadian Catholic culture

### Modern Recognition

The Phaneuf family history is well-researched and documented, with:
- Family associations
- Published genealogies
- Historical studies of New England captives

## Genealogical Notes

- **Farnsworth Family**: The Farnsworths were an established English family in Massachusetts. Matthias's father and grandfather remained in New England.
- **Phaneuf Research**: Well-documented due to interest in New England captive narratives and French-Canadian genealogy
- **DNA Evidence**: Modern DNA studies of Phaneuf descendants confirm English origins
